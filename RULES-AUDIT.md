## v3.2.2 — Narrative adjudication and location rules

- A failed Glassfen rescue check represents a costly **successful evacuation**, not a still-unresolved rescue. It awards no success-only gold and still imposes the HP cost. The rescue-completed state is reconciled in older saves.
- A public mandate is a separate political result from evidence gathered, keeper reputation, or relief supplied. A persuasion success grants the signed mandate; it does not silently award clues, relief or faction standing. Each attempt consumes one day and provisions/fatigue. An unchanged failed offer cannot be spammed; new material support is required before retrying.
- Paying the Waycross guard buys passage but **does not prove** that the toll ledger is fraudulent. Verified evidence, private payment, and other passage methods have separate flags and narration.
- Ending independent stewardship uses original campaign rules: relinquishing earlier private claims consumes settlement time and money, rations or travel HP, with recorded costs. It is not an SRD mechanic.
- Long rests are available at supported waystations or settlements. Foraging is confined to suitable outdoor stages (travelers’ camp, North Moor, Glassfen). Short rests remain broadly usable outside combat. These are DM-specific environmental judgments.
- NPC/companion references depend on who is actually present; no unattached companion is assumed to have intervened.

# Rules audit — Lantern & Key v2.0

This document records the intended tabletop rules baseline, corrections since v1.3 and the known boundaries of the learning simulation. Reference ruleset: **SRD 5.2.1**, corresponding to the 2024 fifth-edition revision, not SRD 5.1 (2014).

**Official source:** https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd (download *SRD 5.2.1*, licensed CC BY 4.0).

## Intentional omissions / approximations

- **Guided branching story**, not open-text free-form roleplay or AI Dungeon Master. “Tabletop mode” hides some advance hints but still uses the same fixed story choices.
- Three preset Level-1/2 characters; not all starting traits (species, backgrounds, feats, Fighting Styles, Weapon Mastery, Wizard spellbook, complete weapon proficiencies and every skill) are available for selection. Those omissions should not be taken as indicating the features do not exist in tabletop D&D.
- Chapter 3 is abstract turn-based combat; Chapter 5 uses an intentionally small grid, deterministic encounter geometry, 30-ft tutorial speed and basic monster decisions. No multiplayer, vertical spaces, diagonal movement variant, spell areas, full line of sight or comprehensive conditions.
- Chapter-5 cover/Hide and target selection are scripted examples to teach the concept, not a general-purpose VTT or rules arbiter.
- Potion drinking from the inventory outside combat is a basic interaction; the Bonus Action distinction applies in combat.
- In Chapter 7, non-Wizard characters **observe the tutor** cast Wizard spells. The training focus illustrates concentration for all classes but does not grant permanent spellcasting.
- A character at 0 HP can retry a nearby story checkpoint; formal unconsciousness, stabilization and death-saving throws are not simulated. This is a **learning safety net**, not the tabletop rule.
- A homemade **smoke pellet** is a narrative/training consumable, not an officially listed standard SRD item. Other items are narrowed to explicit scene uses and do not cover improvised unlimited actions.
- Chapter selection restores snapshots and rewinds later events; it is a replay feature, not an in-world ability to undo time.

## v2.3 follow-up (2026-10-10)

- **Rests:** players may decide to spend a further available Hit Die after each roll on the same uninterrupted Short Rest. Each roll adds the CON modifier, with a minimum of 1 HP.
- **Movement:** each entered 5-ft square costs one movement unit, or two on difficult terrain, with eight-neighbor diagonal movement, tutorial-specific blocked-space restrictions, a least-cost path, and opportunity attacks checked along the route. Explicit freehand path drawing is not yet implemented.
- **Ranged attacks:** disadvantage if an eligible hostile creature is within 5 ft, including spell attack rolls; opposing advantage and disadvantage cancel.
- **Action Surge:** one additional Action on the turn, excluding the Magic action (2024 rules). The revised practice duel now pauses before the sentry responds.
- **Chapter 8 challenge:** a brief training duel and an alternative noncombat resolution to demonstrate new class abilities before the ending. Nonlethal training strikes and simplified NPC behavior are intentional house rules.
- **Character creation:** the preset characters still omit full origin, feat, weapon mastery, and prepared-spell selection. These remain labeled simplifications; this project does not assert full SRD simulation.

## v2.4 — Original artifact, not an official class feature

**The Last Lantern** is a fictional campaign-specific item created for this tutorial, not a named magic item from SRD 5.2.1. Its three sparks, story applications, rest-based replenishment, and distinct ending choices are **homebrew adventure rules**. They are intended to teach resource tradeoffs, roleplay consequences and informed decisions, not to represent an official D&D spell, magic-item template or class feature. Players without the artifact still have appropriate alternatives.

A migration deliberately treats `ending: "take"` as authoritative and grants the carried item even if earlier backups contained `inventory.lantern: 0`; its unique item persists after sparks are spent. New save fields default safely to preserve old data.

Chapter replays are separate from the main campaign, rather than rewinding the main slot.

## v3.1 — The Shattered Watchroad expansion

The campaign now includes milestone advancement from level 2 to level 3 on reaching Glassfen (Chapter 12), and level 4 on reaching the Citadel (Chapter 15). Preset training characters receive additional Hit Points, Hit Dice, and class-specific options. The level-4 primary ability-score increase is preset at +2 instead of asking the player to choose a feat or Ability Score Improvement. The character sheet and sidebar display updated combat resources at these levels.

**Fighter:** The training Champion has improved weapon critical range on a natural 19–20 in the new Watchroad encounters. Second Wind and Action Surge remain available, with in-encounter limitations visible to the player. These changes are an educational subset, not a full Fighting Style or Weapon Mastery simulation.

**Rogue:** Sneak Attack increases to 2d6 at level 3. The Act II Thief has an adventure-specific Fast Hands distraction option for setting up a qualifying attack; this **is not** a complete or literal implementation of the SRD Thief feature. Cunning Action and existing tutorial restrictions remain.

**Wizard:** Level-3/4 wizardry now tracks second-level spell slots separately and adds Shatter (3d8 thunder with a Constitution save for half damage). Damage and enemy AI are intentionally simplified. Arcane Recovery in Act II returns one expended second-level slot if one is available, otherwise a first-level slot; this is a narrowed implementation of the recovery rules, not the complete selection rules for Wizard spell-level recovery.

**Rest and travel:** Act II adds short and long rests, rations or paid lodging, recovery, foraging using Wisdom (Survival), optional companions and fatigue when provisionless. A long rest consumes a ration or 5 gp as a **campaign-specific economy requirement**, not an SRD-wide rule. Map and chapter travel charge time and supplies consistently. A failed obstacle check can still progress the story at a narrative or HP cost; this is an explicit DM choice, not a generic rule guaranteeing success.

**Original narrative mechanics:** Companion trust, faction reputation, seven major challenge gates, waylight ownership, Last Lantern sparks, businesses, bribes, investments, and the ending mandate/charter rules are all original homebrew systems. Nine Act II combat encounters are optional, with alternative solutions. Combatants, battle ranges and resolution are deliberately simplified. These choices teach resource management, negotiation, exploration and causal consequences rather than claiming to replicate every published rule.

**Tests:** See the v3.1 README for the full 125-test result and browser walkthrough scope. The save format retains `version: 1` for compatibility. New state fields have defaults and are bounded and sanitized by save validation/normalization.


## v3.2 — Regional light restoration

The reusable silver wolf-key, self-aligning waylight conductors, lantern sparks, and independent stewardship are fictional campaign-specific mechanisms. They are not part of the official D&D rules. Four choices remain distinct: one spark consumed for a magical public restoration; a reusable key for mechanical public restoration; a day of labor and (when available) one ration for independent stewardship; or private guild control with monetary and reputational consequences. These outcomes are recorded for each waylight and persist through save/load.
